This is the "why" document. It explains what we are trying to achieve, the chain

================================================================================
1. THE GOAL
================================================================================

The thesis is "Implementation and Reinforcement Learning of a Musculoskeletal
Model for Motor Control and Rehabilitation", with a derived IEEE paper.

The scientific claim it needs to support: that a deep RL policy can learn to
control a 9-muscle, 4-DOF arm model to reach targets, and that the resulting
controller can be analysed the way a biological motor system is analysed.

Concretely, the documents promise:

  * A four-algorithm benchmark — SAC, TD3, DDPG, PPO — over 10 seeds each at
    1,000,000 training steps, on an identical environment and evaluation
    protocol, with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests between them.
  * Muscle synergy analysis (non-negative matrix factorisation, variance
    accounted for) on the activation traces the policy produces.
  * A co-contraction index (Falconer & Winter) over the elbow flexor/extensor
    groups.
  * A reward-term ablation.
  * A domain-randomisation robustness breakdown.
  * Non-learning baselines (a PD controller, and random actions) for context.

WHAT "SUBSTANTIAL RESULTS" ACTUALLY MEANS HERE

Not "the pipeline ran". The pipeline has run before and produced nothing usable.
It means: A POLICY THAT ACTUALLY REACHES TARGETS.

This is the crux, and it is worth being blunt about why. If the trained policies
achieve 0% success, then:

  - all four algorithms tie, and every Wilcoxon test returns p = 1.0;
  - there is no algorithm ranking, so the benchmark chapter has no content;
  - synergy analysis on a policy that never completes a reach is meaningless
    (this already happened once: episodes terminated in ~1 step, yielding 6
    activation samples, and report.py correctly refused to compute a synergy
    decomposition from them);
  - the co-contraction index describes a policy doing nothing recognisable;
  - the ablation compares four flavours of failure.

Fifty hours of compute spent on a broken agent produces forty identical zeros.
That is the single most important fact governing the strategy below.

THE PROJECT'S HISTORICAL WEAKNESS, WHICH FRAMES EVERYTHING

An audit on 2026-07-18 established that the thesis's entire quantitative results
section was placeholder data — numbers typed during drafting, generated by
np.random.normal in one case, intended to be replaced by real experiments that
never happened. That has since been rebuilt into a real pipeline, and the
fabrication is gone.

The lesson retained: this project's failure mode is plausible-looking numbers
that do not correspond to anything real. Every working rule in section 4 exists
because of that.


================================================================================
2. THE LOGIC — WHY EACH STEP FOLLOWED FROM THE LAST
================================================================================

The reasoning chain, in order. Each link is a conclusion that forced the next
action.

(a) The grid costs ~50 hours (4 algorithms x 10 seeds x ~78 min). It cannot be
    launched speculatively.
       -> Therefore: one seed must demonstrably learn FIRST. A pilot gates the
          grid. This rule predates today and has been correct every time.

(b) A pilot ran and reported 0% success at 0.295 m final error. The stated
    explanation was "not enough training".
       -> But the control rate was one action per physics step, so a 2-second
          episode was 1000 agent steps and a 1e6 budget bought only ~1000
          reaching attempts. Frame-skip 10 (policy at 50 Hz, physics at 500 Hz)
          buys ~10,000 attempts for the same cost, because wall-clock is ~100%
          gradient updates and physics is nearly free.
       -> This was a correct and useful fix, but it did not solve the problem.

(c) The 50 Hz run was destroyed by a forced Windows Update reboot at ~900k of
    1e6 steps, having saved nothing.
       -> Therefore: no long run is acceptable until it is resumable. Fixed
          before anything else (atomic checkpointing every 50k steps).

(d) The relaunched run finished: 0.215 m, 0% success, and effort at ~48% of
    maximum activation where ~8% holds the arm against gravity.
       -> That effort figure made no physical sense for a reaching task. A
          reaching movement should not cost six times what holding position
          costs. Something other than reaching was going on.

(e) Reading the environment revealed a mechanism: every per-step reward was
    <= 0, and a numerical blow-up terminated the episode with NO penalty. In
    RL, a TERMINATED episode bootstraps nothing — its return from that point is
    exactly 0 — whereas surviving 100 steps of negative reward cost about -20.
       -> Hypothesis: destroying the arm was worth roughly twice the success
          bonus, and far easier. The reward's optimum was self-destruction.

(f) Measured it rather than assuming it. 100% of evaluation episodes ended in a
    deliberate blow-up at step ~7 of 100.
       -> Confirmed. Every behavioural number this project ever produced was
          measuring self-destruction, not reaching. This retroactively explains
          the universal 0% success far better than "insufficient training" did.
          The velocity guard fires silently, so this may have corrupted every
          run from the beginning.

(g) Before rewriting the reward, checked whether the model was simply fragile —
    if the arm exploded under any strong command, the reward would be a red
    herring and the fix would belong in the physics.
       -> It is not fragile. It survives 100/100 steps under full co-activation
          of all nine muscles, and under three other adversarial patterns. So
          the fix belonged in the reward, and no model change was made.

(h) Fixed the reward (v3): a survival offset making every step strictly
    positive, so quitting forfeits value; success no longer terminates, making
    the task reach-AND-hold; an explicit blow-up penalty.
       -> Result: exploit gone, 0% blow-ups, full-length episodes, reward
          -4 -> +317. But accuracy did not improve at all, and effort was
          IDENTICAL to the exploiting policy.

(i) So: necessary but not sufficient. Diagnosed what the fixed policy does.
       -> It tracks targets well (spread ratio 0.80), travels the full required
          distance, reaches ~0.16 m at step 29 — and then DRIFTS BACK OUT to
          0.23 m and never settles. It is simultaneously rigid (co-contraction
          index 0.725) and jittery (3.8x excess path length).
       -> Diagnosis: the reach is roughly right. PRECISION AND SETTLING are
          what is missing. That is a far more tractable problem than "it does
          not learn".

(j) Three candidate causes, all of which cost ~3% of the per-step reward under
    v3 — i.e. the policy had no material reason to avoid any of them: cheap
    effort, cheap jitter, and an error-cost gradient that FLATTENS on approach
    (0.96/m of improvement at 23 cm, only 0.60/m at 5 cm). The policy stops
    improving exactly where improving stops paying.
       -> v4: effort penalty x10, smoothness penalty x10 (and rescaled to be
          comparable), and a new dense precision bonus.

(k) One competing explanation had to be tested first: co-contraction is the
    textbook rational response to UNKNOWN ACTUATOR GAINS, and the agent trains
    under +-20% per-muscle strength randomisation but is evaluated without it.
    If domain randomisation drives the stiffness, no effort penalty removes it
    without also costing the robustness the thesis claims.
       -> Tested with two cheap 120k probes. REFUTED: co-contraction is
          essentially identical with and without randomisation (0.708 vs
          0.727). Randomisation is also mildly helpful on tracking and drift,
          so it stays on.
       -> Unwelcome second finding: raising the effort penalty tenfold did not
          reduce activation either. Suggestive, not conclusive (the probes ran
          120k steps against v3's 1e6; the missing control is a v3-weights
          probe at 120k).

(l) The probes also exposed an error in our own fix: the precision bonus had a
    length scale of 0.05 m, so at the 0.23 m where the policy plateaus it was
    worth 0.03 of a ~4 reward. It did nothing. It only began paying once the
    hand was already inside ~15 cm — the exact region the policy could not
    reach. A prize behind a wall.
       -> Rescaled to 0.15 m, matched to where the policy actually lives.

(m) Pilot 5 launched on that configuration. In progress.


================================================================================
3. THE STRATEGY
================================================================================

THE GATE

One seed must show real learning before the grid runs. The gate is deliberately
NOT "did the number improve". It is evidence the agent is solving the task
rather than gaming it:

    blow_up_rate = 0
    mean_episode_len = 100 (episodes are actually being attempted)
    a monotone fall in error
    plus, for v4: co-contraction index well below 0.7, and drift near zero

Only then does the ~50-hour grid make sense.

THE ESCALATION LADDER

Compute is spent in increasing order of cost, and each rung must justify the
next:

    seconds     — static feasibility and stability tests (oracle_feasibility,
                  test_stability): can the model even do this?
    ~1 minute   — behaviour diagnostics on an existing policy: what is it
                  actually doing?
    ~13 minutes — short probes (120k steps) to compare two configurations
    ~78 minutes — a full single-seed pilot
    ~50 hours   — the grid

Two of today's most important findings cost seconds and minutes respectively:
the model-stability result, and the termination diagnostic.

WHAT WE ARE OPTIMISING FOR

Not the headline error number. A policy that reaches 0.20 m by co-contracting
and jittering is worth less to this thesis than a policy that reaches 0.25 m
with clean reciprocal activation, because the synergy and co-contraction
analyses — two full sections of the document — need activation patterns that
mean something biologically.


================================================================================
4. WORKING RULES ADOPTED
================================================================================

1. MEASURE BEFORE FIXING. The exploit was found by instrumenting a policy, not
   by reading code — the code had been read several times without anyone seeing
   it. Every hypothesis since has been tested before being acted on, including
   the two that turned out to be wrong.

2. INSTRUMENT SO FAILURE MODES CANNOT HIDE. Nothing in the old metrics could
   have revealed that episodes were ending at step 7 of 100: the error number
   looked plausible and the learning curve looked like a plateau. Evaluation
   and the training curve now both report blow_up_rate and mean_episode_len.
   A healthy run is visibly healthy on the face of the results.

3. RULE OUT THE ALTERNATIVE BEFORE THE EXPENSIVE FIX. Checking that the model
   was stable took seconds and determined that the fix belonged in the reward,
   not the physics. Testing the domain-randomisation hypothesis took 26 minutes
   and prevented a wrong and costly change.

4. DISTRUST THE INSTRUMENTS TOO. The first behaviour diagnostic reported every
   final hand position as identical, because DummyVecEnv auto-resets when an
   episode ends. Same class of error as the original bug: a tool producing a
   plausible-looking number that means nothing.

5. MAKE LONG RUNS SURVIVABLE. Checkpoint atomically, resume automatically. A
   forced reboot should cost minutes, not a run.

6. REPORT FAILURES AS FAILURES. Pilot 4 removed the exploit and did not improve
   accuracy; it is recorded that way. The "best" checkpoint scored worse than
   the final one on the test stream; that is recorded too, because it is the
   selection protocol working correctly rather than a result to bury.

7. KEEP THE THESIS TEXT HONEST AGAINST THE CODE. Every behavioural number
   predating 2026-08-14 is void and must not be carried forward. What survives
   is what never depended on policy quality: the Newton-Raphson vs neural
   forward-pass timing benchmark, the parameter count and policy footprint, the
   muscle-function validation, and the model-stability result.

6. THE DECISION AHEAD
================================================================================

When pilot 5 finishes there are three outcomes, with three different responses.

IF IT PASSES THE GATE — accuracy materially improved, co-contraction down,
drift near zero:
    Run the update-pause script, then launch the grid. The pipeline and
    document wiring are already done; only compute is missing.

IF ACCURACY IMPROVES BUT CO-CONTRACTION STAYS AT ~0.7:
    Then the stiffness is structural, not reward-side — most plausibly the arm
    requires co-contraction for stability at this control rate, or the muscle
    set (which lacks the rotator cuff entirely) leaves the shoulder needing
    stiffness to be controllable. That stops being a bug and becomes a FINDING:
    an RL policy independently converging on co-contraction is a real motor
    control phenomenon, and one a rehabilitation-framed thesis can report
    rather than apologise for.

IF ACCURACY IS STILL POOR:
    Stop tuning the reward. Three iterations is enough to conclude the problem
    is elsewhere, and further blind adjustment burns 78 minutes a time. The
    remaining levers, in order of expected value:
      - the privileged oracle reaches <2 cm at some point in only 27% of
        episodes and satisfies the strict success criterion just ~7% of the
        time. That is a low ceiling. The task or the success criterion may
        simply be too hard for this model, and the honest move is to report
        graded accuracy against that measured ceiling rather than a success
        rate that is near-zero by construction.
      - a curriculum: sample near targets first, widen as competence grows.
      - longer training. The curves have not converged; 1e6 may be too few.
      - hyperparameter search. optuna_search.py exists and has never been run.
